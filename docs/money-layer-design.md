## 1. The incident

On 2026-08-10 the dashboard showed **$885** of revenue for a day that actually did **$2,261**. Not a
rounding error — 61% of the day was missing, and the shop noticed because the "Completed Today" column
listed 8 jobs while the revenue card's subtitle claimed 6.

Cause: `dashboard/page.tsx` pulled every completed job from Jan 1 through month end and summed the line
items in JavaScript. PostgREST caps every response at **1000 rows** and reports no error when it
truncates. The shop crossed 1,002 completed jobs a few days earlier, so the query silently started
dropping two of them — and because there was no `ORDER BY`, *which* two was arbitrary.

Measured at the time:

```
completed jobs Jan 1 – Aug 31   : 1,002
rows the query actually returned: 1,000
today's completed jobs          : 8 exist, 6 reached the revenue math
Today's Revenue    shown $   885.00  |  actual $  2,261.00  |  missing $1,376.00
This Month         shown $10,027.00  |  actual $ 11,403.00  |  missing $1,376.00
```

The cap cannot be argued with from the client — verified against the live database:

```
.limit(5000)      -> 1000 rows
.range(0, 4999)   -> 1000 rows
.range(1000,1999) ->    2 rows   (the data is there; the query just can't ask for it in one go)
```

**Fixed same day** by narrowing the range to last-month-start (1,002 → 288 rows) and adding
`assertComplete()`, which requests `{ count: "exact" }` and throws when the row set comes back short.
That stops the bleeding. It does not address why a revenue figure was ever assembled this way.

## 2. Root cause: one fact, three faces

**The database does not know what a job is worth.**

`calculateTotals(lineItems, settings, chargeSalesTax)` in `src/lib/utils/totals.ts:122` is the only
place that math exists. Postgres holds line items and shop settings and has never been told how to
combine them. Everything below follows from that one fact:

**Face 1 — every consumer must fetch raw rows to compute anything.** Dashboard, reports, trends,
receivables and the tax report all pull jobs plus nested line items across the network and sum them in
JS. That is the *only* reason a row cap could ever touch a dollar figure. An aggregate query returns
one row and cannot truncate.

**Face 2 — every consumer re-derives the total differently, and they disagree today.** This is not
hypothetical and does not require a bug:

| Surface | What it calls "the job's value" |
|---|---|
| Job page / footer | `calculateTotals().grandTotal` — labor + parts + supplies + hazmat + tax |
| `receivables.ts:62-64`, `:118-121` | raw `sum(job_line_items.total)`, inspection categories dropped — **no tax, no supplies, no hazmat** |
| `revenue.ts:sumJobRevenue` | raw `sum(total)`, inspection categories dropped — same omissions |
| `reports.ts` tax report | parts only for the taxable base, plus its own revenue sum |

So A/R understates every taxed job by the tax, supplies and hazmat it never adds. Your A/R page and
your job page already disagree about what a customer owes.

**Face 3 — it is what made split-tender expensive.** Constraint 4 of the split-tender review — *"the
grand total is never stored"* — is this same fact seen from the payments side. It is why that design
needed an RPC taking a caller-supplied `p_total`, why a database trigger was impossible, why no SQL
backfill could ever work, and why amendment A3 proposed `jobs.total_snapshot`.

One cause. Fix it once.

## 3. Measured state of every money query (2026-08-10)

Live counts against production. The cap is 1,000:

```
receivables (unpaid complete)                    19   ok
dashboard revenue AFTER today's fix             288   ok
tax report (ALL paid jobs, no date filter)      983   !! 17 rows from the cap
all completed jobs, all time                  1,002   *** already truncating ***
all jobs, all time                            1,038   *** already truncating ***
all job_line_items                            2,305   *** already truncating ***
```

**`getTaxReportData` is the urgent one.** It queries every paid job in the shop's entire history with
**no date filter at all**, then narrows to the requested year in JavaScript (`reports.ts:625`). So the
cap bites before the year filter runs. At ~8 completed jobs a day it crosses 1,000 within days, and
when it does, the DOR filing number starts dropping arbitrary jobs with no error. **This needs a SQL
date filter now, independent of everything else in this document.**

**`customer-insights.ts` is returning wrong numbers today.** `:57-62` fetches all-time completed
jobs with `.limit(50000)` — clamped to 1000 — ordered `date_finished` **ascending**, then builds a
first-visit map from the result. Completed jobs are already at 1,002, so the two newest are dropped
and `newCustomers` / `repeatRate` are computed from an incomplete map. Small today, growing daily.

**`trends.ts:145` carries a comment claiming `// Limit 10000 to override Supabase default 1000-row
cap`.** That is false — `.limit()` is clamped by the cap, not the reverse. A wrong comment here is
worse than none, because it tells the next reader the query is safe.

**The appointments read fails in the worst direction.** `dashboard/page.tsx:145-152` has no date
bound and is ordered `scheduled_at` **ascending**, so on truncation it keeps the oldest rows and
drops the future — "Appointments Today" and "Upcoming Bookings" would render their empty states with
no error. Measured 2026-08-10: 4 confirmed rows, 996 of headroom, so this is years away at current
booking volume — but the failure direction makes it worth bounding cheaply rather than watching.

`receivables.ts` has two further defects found while writing this, unrelated to volume:
- `:48` and `:94` destructure `{ data }` and **discard `error` entirely** — a failed query renders
  **$0 outstanding**, indistinguishable from "everyone has paid." Direct violation of the
  `CLAUDE.md` rule on this.
- `.limit(10000)` on both queries, which we now know does nothing against the cap. It reads as
  protection and provides none.

## 4. Proposed architecture

### Layer 1 — `job_totals`, the canonical money view

One row per job, computed in SQL, matching `calculateTotals` exactly:

```sql
create view job_totals as
select
  j.id                as job_id,
  j.date_finished,
  j.payment_status,
  j.charge_sales_tax,
  labor_total,                    -- sum(total) where type = 'labor'
  parts_total,                    -- sum(total) where type = 'part'
  shop_supplies,                  -- method/rate/cap, scoped by category
  hazmat,                         -- flat amount, scoped by category
  tax_amount,                     -- parts_total * rate, only when charge_sales_tax
  grand_total,                    -- labor + parts + supplies + hazmat + tax
  service_revenue                 -- grand_total excluding inspection-category items
from jobs j ...
```

Note the tax base is **parts only** — not labor, not supplies, not hazmat (`totals.ts:177-181`). The
SQL must reproduce that, along with the category-scoping of both fees (`feeAppliesToJob`) and the
`total ?? quantity * unit_cost` fallback for null totals.

**This must be proven equivalent, not assumed.** A differential test runs `calculateTotals` and the
view over every existing job and asserts they agree to the cent. That test then guards the pair
forever — change one without the other and it fails.

Then:
- Dashboard revenue = `select sum(service_revenue) from job_totals where date_finished between $1 and $2`
  — **one row back. No cap. No JS summing. Cannot truncate.**
- A/R outstanding reads `grand_total` from the same view, which fixes Face 2 for free
- Tax report sums the same view
- Split-tender finally has a total Postgres can see

### Layer 2 — aggregation RPCs, not row fetches

```
get_revenue_summary(p_from date, p_to date)
  returns (service_revenue, job_count, labor, parts, tax, supplies, hazmat)
```

The dashboard stops downloading jobs entirely. Same for `getDailySummary`, the tax report, trends and
receivables. **Rule: a screen that displays a number should not download the rows behind it.**

### Layer 3 — guardrails, because the real defect is that nothing noticed

The bug shipped with no code change. It appeared because a row counter crossed a threshold. Every gate
in the workflow was structurally blind to it:

| Gate | Why it missed this |
|---|---|
| Unit tests | Fixtures are small. Nothing has 1,000 rows |
| `/scoped-review` | There was no diff to review — the code was months old |
| Typecheck / lint | Types were correct. Truncation is a runtime property of data volume |
| `/verify-flow` | Clicking through renders a number; nothing says it's the *wrong* number |

So:
1. **`assertComplete()`** on every remaining row-fetch that feeds a figure.
   *(Helper shipped 2026-08-10 and applied to **1 of 15** such queries — the dashboard revenue read.
   The other 14 are listed in §3 and below; do not read this as done.)*
2. **A volume test** — seed >1,000 jobs, assert every money figure still matches ground truth. This is
   the gate that would have caught it, and the only one that would have.
3. **A daily drift cron** — recompute yesterday's revenue two independent ways (view vs JS) and
   `Sentry.captureException` on mismatch. Mirrors the split-tender review's A20.
4. **A review rule:** any `.select()` feeding a money figure must either aggregate in SQL, or carry
   `{ count: "exact" }` + `assertComplete`. Add to `CLAUDE.md` anti-patterns.

## 5. The hard decision inside Layer 1: settings drift

Shop settings are **inputs** to the total — tax rate, supplies method/rate/cap, hazmat amount, and the
category scoping of both fees. They change over time.

A view that reads *current* settings is correct for open jobs and **wrong for history**: raise the
hazmat fee and last year's revenue silently changes. Re-file a prior quarter and the number won't match
what you filed.

Two options:

**(a) Freeze totals at completion.** When a job is marked complete, write `grand_total` and a
`totals_breakdown` snapshot onto the row. History becomes immutable and cheap to read. Requires
deciding what happens when a completed job is edited.

**(b) Version shop settings.** Timestamp every settings change; the view joins the settings row that
was in effect on `date_finished`. Nothing is frozen, everything is reconstructible, but every read pays
a temporal join and the settings table gets more complex.

**Recommendation: (a).** It matches how the shop actually behaves — a finished job is a finished
transaction — and it is the same decision the split-tender review reached independently as amendment
A3. Making it once serves both projects.

## 7. Sequencing: this comes before split-tender

Both projects need the same thing — the grand total in the database.

Build split-tender first and it gets built against a JS-only total, which is precisely what forced the
caller-supplied `p_total`, its forgery surface, the `total_snapshot` amendment and the line-item lock.
Build the money layer first and a meaningful chunk of that design deletes itself: the RPC stops needing
`p_total` at all, and the balance becomes derivable in SQL.

**Recommendation: money layer first.** Split-tender v3 stays parked until `job_totals` exists.

Rough sizing: Layer 1 + the freeze decision 2–3 days · Layer 2 + migrating six consumers 2–3 days ·
Layer 3 guardrails 1–2 days. **~1–1.5 weeks**, and it makes split-tender smaller rather than just safer.

## The central defect

**§4 and §5/§8 specify two different grand totals and no precedence rule.** §4 proposes a live view
computed from *current* `shop_settings`; §5's recommendation — settled as Decision 1 — freezes a
snapshot onto the job row at completion. The two sections were written three weeks apart and never
reconciled. Every downstream claim (the differential test, the drift cron, the A/R fix, the
split-tender unblock, all six consumer migrations) inherits the ambiguity. An implementer building
from this doc builds one half of each design, and the freeze ends up write-only.

## Findings that landed on decisions settled 2026-08-31

Both of the owner decisions recorded in §8 were found to be under-specified rather than wrong:

- **Decision 1 (freeze) has no firing point.** Five writers produce `status='complete'`, and two of
  them INSERT the job *before* its line items exist — so the obvious trigger freezes **$0.00 onto
  every Quick Pay counter sale**. Decision 2 then locks that $0 permanently. Raised independently
  by 8 findings.
- **Decision 2 (recompute while unpaid, refuse once paid) cites a gate its own model calls
  insufficient.** `setJobChargeSalesTax`'s comment (`jobs.ts:266-272`) says `payment_status` alone
  is not enough; Decision 2 reproduces exactly that half. As written it permits recomputing the
  total of a job whose finalized Stripe invoice is already in the customer's inbox. Raised by 9
  findings. The gate must be written as a predicate, not a gloss.
- **"Freeze" conflates two events** — writing the snapshot (at completion) and making it immutable
  (at settlement). These need two columns, `totals_frozen_at` and `totals_locked_at`; Decision 2 is
  unimplementable while one word means both.

## Blocking prerequisites — nothing gets coded until these are written down

1. A1 — §4 and §8 specify two different grand_totals with no precedence rule. Until §4 states `case when j.totals_frozen_at is not null then <snapshot> else <live> end`, an implementer will build one half of each design and the freeze will be write-only. Every other amendment depends on this sentence existing.

2. A2 — 'Freeze' currently conflates two distinct events: writing the snapshot (at completion) and making it immutable (at settlement). They must become two columns, `totals_frozen_at` and `totals_locked_at`. Decision 2's 'recompute while unpaid' is unimplementable while one word means both.

3. A3 — Decision 2 cites `setJobChargeSalesTax` as its model but reproduces only the half that function's own comment (jobs.ts:266-272) calls insufficient. As written it permits recomputing the total of a job whose finalized Stripe invoice is already in the customer's inbox. The gate must be written as a predicate, not a gloss.

4. A4/A5 — The freeze has no named firing point and no writer inventory. Five writers produce status='complete'; two of them INSERT the job before its line items exist, so the obvious trigger freezes $0.00 onto every Quick Pay counter sale, and Decision 2 then locks that $0 permanently. The trigger design must be settled before schema work.

5. A6 — `service_revenue` as defined ('grand_total excluding inspection-category items') is not today's dashboard revenue; adopting it silently raises every revenue figure by collected sales tax plus both fees, which §9 declares out of scope, and makes §6's stated differential-test oracle fail by construction. The column must be split and named before the SQL is written.

6. A7 — The view and RPCs have no specified security context. A default `create view` in `public` bypasses RLS and is auto-exposed by PostgREST to the browser-shipped anon key. This must land in the same migration as the view, not as a follow-up.

7. A9 — 'Agree to the cent' is not achievable with JS float arithmetic vs Postgres numeric; ~0.4-0.8% of taxed jobs diverge by exactly one cent today. The plan must name numeric authoritative, move calculateTotals to integer cents, and settle where that change sits relative to the freeze — because grandTotal is the amount Stripe is charged, not just a displayed figure.

8. A10 — No backfill exists for the ~1,002 already-complete jobs. Whether history is reconstructed at today's settings (and how that is reconciled against invoices.amount) is a one-time, irreversible decision with a deadline: it stops being correct the moment anyone next edits Rates & Fees.

9. A15 — Whether A/R reads `grand_total` or the inspection-excluding column changes both the amount owed and which jobs appear on the collections screen. This is an owner decision with a dollar figure attached, not an implementation detail.

10. A17 — `shop_settings` is a singleton by convention only. A view joining it unconstrained multiplies every job and doubles every money figure with no error. The singleton migration must land before the view migration.

11. A22 — There is no CI, no supabase/config.toml, and no non-production database; `npx supabase db push` from a laptop hits the one production Postgres, and Vercel deploys separately. §7 sizes days and specifies no deploy order, no rollback, and no cutover discipline for a project that touches every money surface.

## Contradictions reconciled

Fifteen mutual contradictions were found within the surviving set. Four of them — line-item trigger
yes/no, freeze-$0 vs NOT NULL constraint, coalesce vs fail-loud, rounding-fix-first vs freeze-first
— would each have produced a shipped money bug if both halves were implemented as written.

1. C1 — LINE-ITEM TRIGGER, REQUIRED vs FORBIDDEN. A4 (SEC-3, DATA-3, CORR-3, REG-4, FIRS-3) requires an AFTER trigger on `job_line_items` as the only thing that covers insert-then-line-item writers; A5 (SEC-6, CORR-4, DATA-5) says DROP the `job_line_items` trigger because it breaks Quick Pay, fires on cascade deletes, and reverses split-tender-design.md:726. RECONCILE: these are two different triggers doing two different jobs, and the plan must name both. (a) An AFTER INSERT/UPDATE/DELETE *re-freeze* trigger that recomputes the parent's snapshot — required, harmless, never raises. (b) A *refusal* guard that blocks edits to a locked job — this is the one that must NOT be a plain BEFORE trigger: make it DEFERRABLE INITIALLY DEFERRED and key it on `totals_locked_at IS NOT NULL` set in a strictly earlier statement, never on `payment_status='paid'`, and exempt DELETE when the parent job row is itself being deleted. Both objections are then satisfied.

2. C2 — FREEZE AT COMPLETION vs AT SETTLEMENT vs ON PAYMENT-FLIP. §8 D1 and A4 say freeze at completion; COMP-4 says completion is wrong because it freezes the entire 19-job A/R cohort and contradicts 'recompute while unpaid'; FIRS-7 and CORR-10 say payment must also be a freeze trigger because `recordPayment` and the Stripe webhook flip jobs to paid without touching status. RECONCILE: A2's two-column split dissolves this. The snapshot is WRITTEN at whichever comes first — completion or the payment flip — and remains mutable (`totals_locked_at IS NULL`); it becomes IMMUTABLE at settlement. Completion-only freezing and settlement-only freezing are both wrong; first-of-either plus a separate lock is right.

3. C3 — REFUSE-TO-FREEZE-ZERO vs NOT-NULL-ON-COMPLETE. A4 (SEC-3, REG-4) says a complete job with zero line items must leave the frozen total NULL rather than write $0.00; A10 (CORR-10, REG-13, OPER-5) wants a release gate and constraint asserting zero completed jobs have a null frozen total. These cannot both hold: a legitimately empty completed job would fail the constraint forever. RECONCILE: the trip condition is zero LINE ITEMS, not a zero AMOUNT. A comped or all-inspection job legitimately totals $0.00 and freezes normally. Adopt OPER-2's narrower rule: no global raise; leave NULL only when the job has no line items at all, and make the constraint `check (status <> 'complete' or frozen_grand_total is not null or not exists(line items))` — or drop the constraint and monitor the invariant in the drift cron instead. Additionally, the assertion that actually matters is Quick-Pay-specific: frozen total must equal `p_amount_cents/100`, raised inside `record_quick_pay_job`.

4. C4 — COALESCE FROZEN→LIVE vs NEVER COALESCE. A1/A10 (SEC-16, COMP-5, DATA-6, REG-3) put `coalesce(frozen, live)` inside the view so an unfrozen job still reports a correct number; CORR-10 says a coalesce turns a missed backfill or a missed writer into a plausible wrong number instead of a loud failure, which is the exact class of bug the document exists to eliminate. RECONCILE: coalesce, but only where the fallback is provably correct, and make the gap detectable rather than silent. (i) The view coalesces — a NULL frozen total must never render as $0. (ii) The view exposes `is_frozen`. (iii) The invariant 'any job created after <freeze-migration timestamp> that is complete and unfrozen is a bug' is asserted daily by the drift cron and at release. Coalescing to the live branch is safe; coalescing to 0 is not; coalescing without a detector is what CORR-10 is actually objecting to.

5. C5 — ROUNDING FIX FIRST vs FREEZE FIRST. A9 (DATA-15, FIRS-15, OPER-14) wants calculateTotals moved to integer cents before the view, so equivalence is true by construction; CORR-1 says doing that first silently restates already-captured Stripe charges on ~0.8% of paid jobs, because charge-card-on-file.ts:146, create-invoice.ts:146, webhooks/route.ts:588 and receipt/[token]/page.tsx:89 all recompute at render/charge time with no stored total. RECONCILE: CORR-1's ordering wins, and A9's content survives inside it. Freeze columns + backfill at as-charged values (P1) → money-out and print paths read the frozen snapshot (P2) → THEN switch calculateTotals to integer cents (P3), applying to unfrozen and future jobs only → then build the view and run the differential test over new-rule jobs (P4). Frozen history is exempt by construction.

6. C6 — A/R READS `grand_total` vs THE INSPECTION-EXCLUDING COLUMN. CORR-11, COMP-15, DATA-2 and REG-9 say `grand_total` — an unpaid inspection is genuinely owed, and the inspection exclusion exists only to avoid double-counting against `calcInspectionRevenue`, a revenue-recognition concern that does not apply to receivables. FIRS-8 says the exclusion is deliberate and A/R must keep it. RECONCILE: this is an owner decision, not an engineering one, and A15 frames it as such with both numbers computed in advance (19 rows — one query). The engineering constraint is only that both A/R surfaces (`getReceivablesSummary` and `getFleetARSummary`) resolve to the same column, since they already disagree today.

7. C7 — THIRD CRON SLOT vs FOLD INTO HEALTH. SEC-19 says register `/api/cron/totals-drift` in vercel.json after confirming the plan tier allows a third; OPER-12 says do not — vercel.json is at 2 of 2 Hobby slots and a third entry has already caused a silent non-registration outage at this shop. RECONCILE: fold the drift check into the existing `/api/cron/health` route (A20). It inherits `requireCronSecret`, the Sentry tag, and the console heartbeat for free, adds no slot, and avoids the known failure. Keep SEC-19's deploy-probe checklist verbatim regardless of which route wins; note the schedule consequence (14:00 UTC means 'yesterday' must be computed in ET).

8. C8 — SINGLETON INDEX AND JOIN FORM. `((true))` (CORR-15, COMP-16) vs `((id is not null))` (SEC-14, DATA-13); and `cross join (select * from shop_settings order by created_at, id limit 1)` (SEC-14, CORR-15) vs plain `left join shop_settings on true` (DATA-13, COMP-16). RECONCILE: use `((id is not null))` — identical semantics, no reliance on a constant index expression that could not be verified here, so it is strictly the safer of two equivalent forms. On the join, DATA-13 is right ONCE the index exists: `limit 1` inside a derived table masks a violated invariant instead of surfacing it. Adopt `left join shop_settings on true` plus the pre-flight count guard plus the block-delete trigger; LEFT (not CROSS) so an empty settings table yields NULL rather than silently dropping every job row.

9. C9 — SPLIT-TENDER UNBLOCKS WHEN? REG-18 says the moment Layer 1 exists and the differential test is green; DATA-20 says only after three contract guarantees are verified in production. RECONCILE: DATA-20 is right and REG-18's version is too loose. §7's own argument is that split-tender's RPC 'stops needing p_total' — which is only true when EVERY job reaching checkout already carries a non-null frozen total. That requires the freeze trigger covering all writers plus the backfill, i.e. the end of P2, not the existence of a view. State the three guarantees explicitly in §7 as the handoff contract.

10. C10 — THE VOLUME TEST IS THE ONLY GATE THAT WOULD HAVE CAUGHT IT vs IT CANNOT BE BUILT. §4 Layer 3 item 2 makes the >1,000-job seeded test load-bearing; SEC-10 and REG-14 note there is no config.toml, no local stack, no CI and no seeding harness, and §7's 1-2 day Layer 3 budget does not include standing one up; OPER-11 and FIRS-14 say build it as a budgeted Phase 0. RECONCILE: split by infrastructure need. The differential test needs NO new infra — it is a read-only `npx tsx` script in the existing scripts/ pattern using fetchAllRows. The volume test genuinely needs Docker + config.toml + a seed generator + a second vitest project + first-ever CI. Budget it separately and let the owner fund it or not — but if it is not funded, strike the 'only gate that would have caught it' claim from §4 rather than leaving a load-bearing gate the budget cannot build. Note also that the volume test must never be seeded into the production project, which is the only database this repo can currently reach.

## Gate verdict (verbatim)

Substantially hardened, but not bulletproof — and five of the residual problems are contradictions introduced by the amendment set itself, not carried over from the original document.

The amendments fix the plan's structural defects convincingly: the two-grand_totals ambiguity (A1/A2), the freeze-writer inventory (A4), the security clauses the original view was missing entirely (A7), the PostgREST-aggregate impossibility (A8), and the backfill the plan never mentioned (A10). Those were the things that would have sunk the project outright, and they are now specified at implementation grain.

What is not settled is the interaction between amendments written against different sections. Concretely: (1) A10 requires the backfill to compute from the view's live branch, while A22 schedules the backfill in P1 and the view in P4 — as ordered, P1 must hand-roll the third face of the math A10 exists to forbid. (2) A1 forbids any consumer from naming `jobs.grand_total_frozen`; A11 instructs seven money-out sites to read exactly that. (3) A4 mandates a RAISE-on-mismatch assertion inside `record_quick_pay_job`, which I verified runs only after the card is captured (called from `/api/terminal/status` and the Stripe webhook, both idempotent retries of a deterministic input) — a deterministic assertion failure there never heals and permanently loses the record of collected money. A5's own rationale names this hazard in passing and A4 mandates the thing that causes it. (4) A9 explicitly warns that `Math.round` is half-toward-+Infinity and then prescribes `floor((num + den/2)/den)`, which has the same defect; verified divergence at partsCents = -8. (5) A4's Quick-Pay-freezes-at-collected-amount rule makes A20/C15's check (ii) permanently red on every counter sale, and a detector that is red every day gets muted — the exact failure C15 was written to prevent.

Beyond the contradictions, three verified gaps remain: no row-level locking is specified anywhere in the freeze/lock path, so a concurrent line-item edit can strand a locked job whose snapshot no longer matches its items and which Decision 2 makes unrepairable; SQL `sum()` skips NULLs, so A17's "NULL never 0" protects a single row read but lets an aggregate silently understate when a job is unfrozen or the settings row is missing; and three amendments (A2, A18, A22) depend on writing audit rows to a table that does not exist in any migration.

None of these is a reason to abandon the plan. All are a reason not to start P1 until they are written down — several are one-sentence fixes, and two of them (the Quick Pay RAISE, the missing FOR UPDATE) write wrong or missing numbers onto money that has already been collected, which is precisely the class of harm this project exists to prevent.
